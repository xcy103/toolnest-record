## Overview

Completed the first task in the Dates + Local Data wave: the **Date Add/Subtract Calculator**.
ToolNest now has 41 tools. The new utility adds or subtracts days, weeks, months or years from a
selected date, runs entirely in the browser and is available in English and Chinese.

## Completed Work

### 1. Date-Only Calendar Arithmetic

Added a shared date-arithmetic module with strict `YYYY-MM-DD` parsing, Gregorian leap-year rules
and a supported range of years 0001–9999. Day and week calculations use UTC calendar fields rather
than local elapsed milliseconds, so daylight-saving transitions cannot shift the result by a day.

Month and year calculations use an explicit clamp rule. When the target month does not contain the
original day, the result becomes the last valid day: for example, January 31 plus one month becomes
February 28 or 29. The page tells users when this adjustment happened instead of applying it
silently.

### 2. Complete Interactive Tool

Added `/date-calculator` with a starting-date field, Today shortcut, Add/Subtract segmented control,
integer amount input and day/week/month/year selector. Results include a locale-formatted full date,
an ISO date, a plain-language calculation summary and copy support. Invalid amounts and calculations
outside the supported year range produce localized messages.

The tool was registered in the Time category, which automatically adds it to search, category and
all-tools navigation plus the bilingual sitemap. A server layout supplies localized metadata,
canonical and alternate-language links, Open Graph fields and `SoftwareApplication` JSON-LD.

## Learning Notes

- Calendar dates and elapsed time are different domains. Adding `86,400,000` milliseconds can be
  wrong across a daylight-saving boundary, while UTC calendar-field arithmetic preserves the date.
- JavaScript's date constructors treat years 0–99 specially; setting the UTC full year explicitly
  avoids accidentally interpreting year 24 as 1924.
- Month addition needs a product rule for dates such as January 31. Clamping to the target month's
  final day is predictable only when the interface explains it.
