## Catch-Up Work

### 1. Explicit Date-Range Rules

Added `/business-days` to count working dates between a starting and ending date. The starting date
is always excluded, while a checkbox controls whether the ending date is included when it is a
workday. Reversed ranges produce an error instead of silently changing the input order.

The result separates business days, weekend days skipped and total dates considered, and states the
inclusion rule in plain language. The core calculation counts complete weeks plus the remaining
dates, so even a range spanning many years does not require scanning every date.

### 2. Configurable Weekends Without a Server

Added a shared seven-day weekend selector, defaulting to Saturday and Sunday. Users can select from
zero to six weekend days, supporting schedules such as Friday/Saturday weekends or seven-day work
weeks. Selecting all seven is prevented because no working date could then be produced.

The first version deliberately excludes public holidays. That keeps the tool browser-only and avoids
a country, region and holiday-data service that would require ongoing updates or backend work.

### 3. Bilingual Product Integration

Added complete English and Chinese copy, localized metadata, canonical and alternate-language links,
Open Graph fields and structured data. Registering the tool in the Time category also adds it to
search, category pages, the all-tools directory and the bilingual sitemap.

## Learning Notes

- Date-range calculators need a precise inclusion rule. Naming the rule in the interface prevents
  two reasonable interpretations from producing apparently conflicting answers.
- A seven-day repeating schedule can be counted as complete weeks plus at most six remaining dates,
  which keeps long-range calculations fast.
